Payment terms are not just a percentage on a quotation. They decide how much money is exposed at each point in the order and what evidence the buyer can still require before releasing the next payment.

A familiar formula such as “30% deposit, 70% before shipment” can work, but the percentages alone say very little. The useful questions are: **who receives the money, what does each payment authorize, what proof is due, and what happens if the goods fail inspection?**

## What payment terms are common when buying from China?

Buyers often encounter a deposit before materials or production and a balance before shipment. The exact structure varies with tooling, custom materials, order value, supplier leverage, product resale value, and the history between the parties.

Common arrangements include:

- full payment before production for samples or very small orders;
- a deposit followed by a balance after production evidence or inspection;
- tooling payments split across design approval, trial, and acceptance;
- staged payments tied to measurable production milestones;
- a letter of credit or another bank-supported structure for suitable transactions;
- open-account terms after a stable trading history and credit approval.

No single arrangement is automatically “standard” or safe. A low deposit to the wrong entity can be riskier than a larger, well-controlled payment to a verified contracting company.

## Verify the beneficiary before discussing percentages

Start with identity. The beneficiary name, contracting supplier, invoice issuer, and verified legal company should fit one documented commercial structure.

Before the first transfer:

1. Complete the [Chinese supplier verification workflow](/blog/verify-chinese-supplier/).
2. Obtain payment instructions on a controlled supplier document.
3. Compare the account name with the signed purchase order or contract.
4. Confirm bank details through a known contact and a second channel.
5. Require internal approval by someone other than the person who entered the payment.
6. Save the verified beneficiary record with the order documents.

A bank account change deserves a fresh check, even if the request appears inside an existing email thread. Compromised mailboxes and look-alike domains can make a fraudulent instruction appear routine.

An unexplained personal account, cryptocurrency request, last-minute country change, or unrelated third-party beneficiary is a stop signal. Do not solve an identity problem by negotiating a smaller transfer.

## Separate tooling, materials, production, and goods

A supplier may ask for a large deposit because several different costs begin before finished goods exist. Ask for the commercial logic instead of arguing only about the percentage.

Break the requirement into:

- custom tooling or fixtures;
- non-returnable raw materials;
- printed packaging or branded components;
- setup and engineering work;
- mass-production conversion cost;
- finished-goods value;
- freight, testing, inspection, or other pass-through charges.

This breakdown shows which amounts are genuinely committed early and which can wait. It also helps the buyer decide whether tooling should use its own acceptance schedule or whether common materials can be ordered in smaller releases.

## Tie every payment milestone to evidence

“Pay 40% when production is halfway complete” is weak because halfway is easy to claim and hard to verify. A better milestone identifies an observable event and a document owner.

| Payment event | Evidence worth requesting | Question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial deposit | Signed order pack, verified beneficiary, approved specification | Is the correct entity authorized to start the correct order? |
| Tooling progress | Trial report, measurements, sample images, change log | Does the tool produce the agreed geometry and finish? |
| Material commitment | Purchase evidence, lot identity, incoming checks where relevant | Were the specified materials secured for this order? |
| Production milestone | Dated output records, in-process checks, lot traceability | Does physical progress match the schedule claim? |
| Balance release | Passed inspection, closed rework, final packing list | Is the completed lot ready for the agreed shipping handoff? |

Evidence does not need to become a paperwork contest. It should answer the specific risk created by the next payment.

## Keep the final payment gate usable

The buyer loses leverage when the balance is paid before the finished lot can be checked. If a pre-shipment inspection is part of the control plan, write the sequence clearly:

1. production and agreed packing reach inspection readiness;
2. the supplier confirms the presented lot and quantity;
3. the inspection is completed against the controlled specification;
4. any failure is contained, corrected, and reinspected when required;
5. the buyer gives written release;
6. the balance becomes due under the agreed terms;
7. shipment proceeds under the named delivery rule.

The [pre-shipment inspection guide](/blog/pre-shipment-inspection-china/) explains what the inspection should cover. If the contract requires full payment before an inspector can see the goods, the inspection is no longer an effective payment gate.

## Handle a failed inspection before it happens

The order documents should say what follows a failed inspection. At minimum, cover containment, rework or replacement, evidence of correction, repeat inspection, timing, and who pays the additional cost.

Do not treat “supplier will fix it” as a release condition. The buyer needs a corrected lot and fresh evidence. If only the sampled defects are repaired, the remaining lot may still contain the same process problem.

For a commercially acceptable deviation, record the affected quantity, concession, price or warranty treatment, and whether the product specification changes for future orders.

## Protect sample and tooling payments

Samples and tooling need their own logic.

For samples, record what the fee covers, which revisions are included, freight responsibility, and whether any amount is credited against a later order. A paid sample is still an evaluation unit, not proof that the production process is ready.

For tooling, define:

- design inputs and approval revision;
- trial stages and included adjustments;
- measurable acceptance criteria;
- ownership and identification markings;
- storage, maintenance, and access;
- limits on relocation or use for another customer;
- treatment if the supplier relationship ends.

Legal ownership language requires appropriate counsel, but operational acceptance still needs dimensions, output, material, finish, and trial evidence.

## Can buyers use escrow, cards, or letters of credit?

The payment method should match the transaction rather than serve as a substitute for supplier verification.

Marketplace protection or escrow may help only when the actual order, specification, communication, and payment remain inside the protected process and the dispute rules cover the problem. A credit card may offer a dispute mechanism, but limits, evidence requirements, timing, and eligible transactions vary.

Letters of credit can exchange payment against specified documents. They do not automatically prove product quality, and poorly drafted document conditions can create cost without controlling the main manufacturing risk. Use banks and qualified trade professionals for a structure appropriate to the countries and transaction.

## Build a payment-control record

For each transfer, retain:

- approved purchase order and contract revision;
- supplier invoice;
- verified beneficiary details and verification date;
- milestone evidence;
- inspection or acceptance decision where applicable;
- internal approver;
- transfer confirmation;
- later changes and the person who authorized them.

This record helps prevent duplicate payments, exposes unexplained changes, and makes later reconciliation or dispute work less dependent on chat history.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a normal deposit for a Chinese supplier?

There is no universal percentage that fits every order. Custom tooling and non-returnable materials may justify earlier funding, while repeat products and stable relationships may support lower deposits or later payment. Judge the amount against verified identity, actual early costs, product resale value, and the evidence available before the balance.

### Should I pay the balance before goods leave China?

Many suppliers request payment before shipment. The buyer can still make the balance conditional on an inspection-ready lot, a passed inspection or accepted corrective action, complete documents, and written release. The sequence must be agreed before the deposit.

### Is a company account always safe?

No. A company account can still belong to the wrong or an unexplained entity. Match the beneficiary to the contracting structure and verify any change independently.

### Can a trading company receive payment for a factory order?

It can be commercially legitimate, but the roles must be disclosed and documented. Confirm which entity contracts, receives funds, controls production, owns tooling, handles export, and remains responsible for nonconforming goods.

## Payment terms should describe control, not trust

Good payment terms do not assume that one side will behave badly. They make the order easier to operate: each party knows the next deliverable, the evidence required, and the event that releases money.
