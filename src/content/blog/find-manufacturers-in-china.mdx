Finding a manufacturer is not the same as finding a company that can send a quotation. A useful sourcing process must answer three separate questions: **Can this supplier legally transact? Can it repeatedly make the required product? Can it do so under commercial terms your business can control?**

The process below is designed for an overseas buyer preparing a first production order. It works best when you treat supplier discovery as evidence collection rather than a race to the lowest unit price.

## 1. Define the product before searching

Start with a one-page product requirement. It should be specific enough for two suppliers to quote the same thing.

Include:

- product function and intended user;
- dimensions, materials, colors, finish, and tolerances;
- packaging, labeling, barcode, and carton requirements;
- target order quantity and estimated annual volume;
- destination country and target shipping date;
- required tests, standards, or certifications;
- reference drawings, photographs, or an approved physical sample;
- which requirements are mandatory and which can be negotiated.

Do not send only a marketplace link and ask for “the same product.” That creates room for silent substitutions. A quote can look cheaper because the supplier assumed thinner material, different packaging, a generic component, or no compliance testing.

For a custom product, version the specification. Put a date and revision number on it. When a requirement changes, record who approved the change and request an updated quotation.

## 2. Choose search channels deliberately

There is no single best source of manufacturers. Use at least two channels so that your shortlist does not depend on one platform’s ranking system.

Common channels include:

1. **B2B marketplaces.** Efficient for building an initial list and understanding common product configurations. A marketplace badge is a lead, not conclusive verification.
2. **Trade fairs and exhibitor directories.** Useful when you need to inspect samples, compare construction, and meet technical staff. Confirm afterward which legal entity will sign and receive payment.
3. **Industry associations and cluster research.** Helpful for products concentrated in a particular manufacturing region.
4. **Customs and shipment databases.** They can suggest experience in a category, but shipment records do not prove current capacity, quality, or authorization to sell a branded design.
5. **Referrals.** Ask what product was made, when, at what volume, and whether the referring buyer has a commercial relationship with the supplier.

Search using process terms as well as product names. For example, add the material, forming process, surface treatment, or relevant manufacturing cluster. This helps separate factories with the required equipment from general trading companies.

## 3. Build a longlist, then remove obvious mismatches

A useful longlist may contain 15–30 candidates. Record the legal company name, location, website or profile, main product line, stated processes, export markets, years operating, and the person who responded.

Remove candidates when:

- their main product line is unrelated to your requirement;
- the company identity changes between profile, quotation, bank account, and email signature;
- they refuse to provide a Chinese legal name or business-license copy;
- product photographs appear copied and the supplier cannot explain the manufacturing process;
- the claimed capacity is inconsistent with visible equipment or staffing;
- answers remain generic after you ask technical questions.

Being a trading company is not automatically a problem. A capable trading partner may coordinate multiple processes well. The important point is disclosure: know who manufactures, who controls quality, who owns tooling, and which entity is contractually responsible.

## 4. Send a structured RFQ

Send the same request for quotation (RFQ) package to each candidate. Ask suppliers to separate, rather than blend, the following:

- unit price at two or three quantity levels;
- tooling, mold, setup, or development charges;
- sample price and sample lead time;
- production lead time after approval and deposit;
- packaging and labeling costs;
- quoted Incoterms rule and named place;
- quotation validity and payment terms;
- proposed material or component deviations;
- testing and inspection costs;
- gross weight, carton dimensions, and units per carton.

Write the Incoterms rule precisely—for example, “FOB Yantian, Incoterms® 2020”—rather than using “FOB price” alone. The [International Chamber of Commerce](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/incoterms-rules/) publishes the Incoterms rules; they allocate specified tasks, costs, and risks, but do not replace a sales contract.

Use the first quotation round to judge communication quality. A supplier that identifies missing specifications and explains cost drivers is often more useful than one that instantly sends the lowest number.

## 5. Compare evidence, not just price

Create a comparison table with weighted criteria. A starting structure is:

| Area | Evidence to request |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Business license, legal name, registered address, payment beneficiary |
| Product fit | Similar products, process explanation, equipment list, engineering feedback |
| Capacity | Production lines, monthly output assumptions, peak-season plan |
| Quality | Inspection records, calibration control, defect handling, traceability |
| Commercial | Price breakdown, MOQ, lead time, payment terms, tooling ownership |
| Compliance | Test scope, responsible laboratory, material declarations, destination-market experience |

A certificate should be checked for scope, issuer, model, standard, issue date, and holder. Do not assume a PDF labeled “CE” or “FDA” applies to the exact product you plan to import.

## 6. Verify the legal entity and operating site

Match the Chinese company name and unified social credit code on the business license with official records in China’s [National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System](https://www.gsxt.gov.cn/index.html). The interface may require Chinese-language searching and access can vary by location.

Then verify the operating reality. Depending on order risk, use a live video walkthrough, independent on-site audit, or your own visit. Ask to see receiving, production, in-process inspection, final inspection, nonconforming-product control, warehouse, and relevant machinery—not only a showroom.

Confirm that the beneficiary on the payment instruction belongs to the contracted entity or that any different arrangement is documented and independently verified. Treat a last-minute bank-account change as a stop event until confirmed through a known communication channel.

## 7. Run samples as a controlled test

A sample is valuable only when you can connect it to the future production standard.

Record:

- supplier and sample date;
- material and component versions;
- measurements and test results;
- approved visual limits;
- deviations and corrective changes;
- the final “golden sample” identifier.

For custom development, separate a visual prototype from a pre-production sample made with production materials and tooling. Passing one handmade sample does not prove mass-production capability.

## 8. Place a controlled first order

The first order should test the system without exposing the business to unnecessary inventory or cash risk. Use a written purchase order or contract that references the approved specification, sample, packaging artwork, inspection criteria, Incoterms rule, delivery schedule, change-control process, and remedies for nonconformity.

Arrange inspection before the balance payment when practical. Retain copies of quotations, approvals, test reports, shipping documents, and payment confirmations in one order file.

## A practical decision rule

Choose the supplier that presents the best **risk-adjusted offer**, not the lowest line-item price. A slightly higher quote may be cheaper after you account for rework, delays, inconsistent packaging, inspection failures, air freight, or unusable stock.

The goal is not to eliminate uncertainty. It is to turn unknowns into named checks, documented evidence, and decision points before the order becomes expensive to change.
