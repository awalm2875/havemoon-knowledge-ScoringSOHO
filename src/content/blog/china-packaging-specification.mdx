A packaging specification for a Chinese manufacturer should define more than box artwork. It must explain how one saleable unit is protected, counted, labelled, packed into inner and master cartons, handled in transit, and verified before shipment.

Packaging failures can create product damage, incorrect quantities, warehouse rejection, relabelling costs, or compliance problems even when the manufactured product itself is acceptable. Treat packaging as part of the controlled product.

## Define the complete packaging hierarchy

Start by mapping every level that applies:

1. product protection in direct contact with the item;
2. individual retail or unit pack;
3. accessories, inserts, and instruction pack;
4. inner pack or display pack;
5. master shipping carton;
6. pallet, crate, or other transport unit;
7. container loading or parcel-shipping configuration.

For each level, assign a packaging code and revision. State the quantity relationship, such as units per inner pack, inner packs per master carton, and cartons per pallet.

Do not assume the supplier's standard export carton matches your warehouse, courier, retailer, or destination-market requirements.

## Control materials and construction

Identify the material and construction required for every pack component.

Depending on the product, specify:

- bag, film, foam, pulp, paperboard, corrugated board, wood, or other material;
- grade, thickness, weight, flute, density, coating, or barrier property where important;
- recycled-content or restricted-substance requirement when applicable;
- sealing method, tape, glue, staples, straps, or fasteners;
- inserts, dividers, corner protection, void fill, desiccant, or corrosion protection;
- contact-material constraints for food, cosmetics, medical, or sensitive products;
- allowed suppliers or approved equivalents for critical packaging.

If a material attribute matters, define how it will be verified. “Strong carton” is not a specification. A board construction, dimension, compression requirement, approved sample, and pack-out test provide clearer evidence.

## Record dimensions, tolerances, and packed weight

For each packaging level, include:

- internal and external dimensions;
- dimension order, such as length × width × height;
- measurement unit and tolerance;
- net and gross weight target or limit;
- maximum empty space or movement where relevant;
- orientation and opening direction;
- closure position;
- pallet footprint, height, overhang, and stacking pattern if used.

Use the final packed configuration to produce booking data. Freight can be re-rated when actual carton dimensions or gross weight differ from estimates, especially for air, courier, and less-than-container-load shipments.

## Separate structural design from artwork

The dieline or packaging drawing should identify cut lines, folds, glue areas, print-safe areas, barcode quiet zones, label positions, and orientation. Artwork should use the approved dieline revision.

Control:

- brand marks and colors;
- product name, model, variant, and quantity;
- barcode type, data, size, contrast, and placement;
- country-of-origin marking where required;
- importer, responsible-person, warning, disposal, or certification information where applicable;
- batch, date, serial, or traceability field;
- language and translation revision;
- shipping marks and handling symbols;
- print method, finish, and acceptable registration or color variation.

Do not copy a compliance mark into artwork merely because a competitor displays it. Confirm that the product and responsible party meet the conditions for using the mark in the destination market.

Use the [product compliance checklist](/blog/china-product-compliance-checklist/) to assign regulatory decisions before artwork is frozen.

## Create one pack-out instruction

A packing team needs a visual sequence, not a collection of disconnected artwork files.

The pack-out instruction should show:

1. product preparation and protective materials;
2. accessory and document count;
3. orientation inside the unit pack;
4. sealing method;
5. unit-pack label and code position;
6. inner-pack quantity and orientation;
7. master-carton quantity and arrangement;
8. carton closure and strapping;
9. shipping marks and labels;
10. pallet or loading pattern where required.

Include photographs or diagrams from an approved packaging sample after sensitive or temporary information is removed. Give the instruction a revision and reference it in the [purchase order pack](/blog/china-purchase-order-checklist/).

## Design for the real distribution route

Packaging should reflect the hazards between the factory and end user. Map the actual route:

- factory handling and temporary storage;
- truck movement to a port, airport, warehouse, or consolidator;
- container, air, rail, parcel, or multimodal carriage;
- palletization or floor loading;
- customs or inspection handling;
- destination deconsolidation;
- warehouse conveyor and racking;
- parcel delivery or retail shelf;
- customer return route where relevant.

Identify likely compression, vibration, impact, puncture, moisture, temperature, abrasion, tampering, and misorientation risks. Select packaging controls and tests for those risks rather than requesting a generic “export standard.”

## Define packaging tests and acceptance criteria

Testing should state the packed configuration, conditioning, method, severity, sample quantity, and pass criteria.

Possible evaluations include:

- visual and dimensional inspection;
- package count and weight verification;
- barcode scan and data check;
- print adhesion, abrasion, or rub resistance;
- seal or closure integrity;
- drop or impact sequence;
- vibration or transport simulation;
- carton compression or stacking evaluation;
- moisture or climate exposure;
- pallet stability;
- product inspection after the packaging test.

A pass result should address both package condition and product protection. A carton can look acceptable while the product, finish, accessory arrangement, or label has failed.

For regulated, dangerous, fragile, high-value, or retailer-controlled products, use the required official, carrier, platform, or customer method rather than inventing a simplified substitute.

## Control wood packaging and destination rules

Solid-wood packaging used in international trade may be subject to phytosanitary requirements. Confirm whether the selected material and route require treatment, marking, or documentation under the destination country's implementation of [ISPM 15](https://www.ippc.int/en/core-activities/standards-setting/ispms/).

Country-of-origin, recycling, material, warning, language, food-contact, dangerous-goods, and producer-responsibility rules vary by product and market. Assign an owner to verify each requirement with the relevant authority before printing.

Do not rely on the packaging factory to decide importer obligations unless the responsibility, evidence, and current rule source are explicitly agreed.

## Approve a packaging sample before mass printing

The packaging sample stage should review structure, fit, protection, print, data, pack-out, and shipping configuration.

Use the [product sample approval workflow](/blog/order-product-samples-from-china/) and add packaging-specific checks:

- dieline and artwork revision match;
- product fits without unintended movement or pressure;
- accessories and instructions are complete;
- barcode data scans correctly;
- colors, finish, print position, and legibility are acceptable;
- labels adhere to the intended surface;
- closure survives expected handling;
- inner and master quantities are correct;
- packed dimensions and weights are recorded;
- required tests are complete;
- approved deviations are documented.

Keep a controlled reference when practical, but preserve numeric dimensions, artwork files, barcode data, materials, and tests in writing.

## Inspect packaging during production and before shipment

Packaging should appear in the production control plan. Early checks can catch a wrong print revision or carton construction before the complete order is packed.

At pre-shipment inspection, verify a defined sample of:

- unit, inner, and master quantities;
- packaging materials and dimensions;
- product and accessory orientation;
- seals, tape, straps, and closures;
- artwork, labels, barcodes, and shipping marks;
- carton condition and gross weight;
- pack-out consistency;
- pallet or loading configuration;
- evidence for required package tests.

Add these points to the [pre-shipment inspection checklist](/blog/pre-shipment-inspection-china/) rather than treating packaging as a final visual glance.

## Packaging specification checklist

Before approval, confirm that the pack defines:

- every packaging level and quantity relationship;
- controlled material and construction;
- dimensions, tolerances, and packed weight;
- dieline, artwork, barcode, label, and language revisions;
- protection method and pack-out sequence;
- destination and customer requirements;
- shipping marks and traceability;
- test methods and pass criteria;
- approved sample reference;
- production checks and final inspection;
- change-control authority;
- final freight-booking data.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who should create the packaging specification?

The buyer should own the required outcome and destination constraints. The product supplier and packaging supplier can contribute manufacturing details, but responsibilities, revisions, verification, and approval should remain explicit.

### Can I use the supplier's standard export carton?

Yes, if its material, dimensions, quantity, marks, protection, handling performance, and route suitability are reviewed and accepted. “Standard” alone does not define those properties.

### When should packaging artwork be finalized?

After the structural dieline, product data, destination requirements, translations, and responsible-party information are controlled. Freeze it early enough for proofing and production, but not before the required content is verified.

### Does a drop test prove the packaging is sufficient?

No single test represents every route hazard. Use a risk-based combination of structural review, pack-out inspection, relevant tests, and actual shipment feedback.

> This guide is general educational information. Packaging, marking, dangerous-goods, environmental, customs, and product requirements vary by jurisdiction and route; confirm current requirements with the relevant authorities, carriers, customers, and qualified professionals.
