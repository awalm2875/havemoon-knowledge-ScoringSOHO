Shipping documents are not a clerical afterthought. They carry the product description, quantities, values, parties, route, and delivery instructions that different people use for booking, export, import, payment, warehouse receiving, and later reconciliation.

A document can look complete and still be wrong for the shipment. The buyer’s job is to make the documents agree with the controlled order and with the destination-market process.

## Start with one shipment record

Create a shipment record before the cargo-ready date. Give it a purchase-order number, product revision, shipment lot, and responsible owner.

Record:

- seller, buyer, exporter, importer, and notify-party roles;
- factory and cargo-ready address;
- product models, variants, and quantities;
- carton or pallet count, dimensions, and gross weight;
- commercial value and currency;
- planned mode, route, and delivery rule;
- destination port, airport, warehouse, or delivery address;
- document deadlines and booking reference;
- inspection or release status.

The [China purchase order checklist](/blog/china-purchase-order-checklist/) provides the upstream control. The shipment record should not introduce a different product name or quantity simply because a forwarder uses another template.

## Commercial invoice

The commercial invoice supports the transaction value and identifies the goods for customs and other parties. Depending on the transaction, it commonly includes:

- seller and buyer legal names and addresses;
- invoice number and date;
- purchase-order or contract reference;
- detailed product description;
- model, SKU, material, and intended use where relevant;
- quantity, unit price, line total, currency, and total value;
- country of origin;
- package count and gross or net weight where requested;
- delivery rule and named place;
- payment or valuation notes required by the broker or authority.

“Gift,” “sample,” “parts,” or a marketplace title is not a substitute for an accurate description. The description should let the broker and authority understand what the item is without guessing from a brand name.

Do not lower or change a value to make duties look smaller. The correct valuation treatment depends on the transaction and destination rules; ask a qualified customs professional when the invoice value, assists, royalties, tooling, samples, or related-party pricing require analysis.

For U.S.-bound shipments, Customs and Border Protection's official [Importing into the United States](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Importing%20into%20the%20U.S.pdf) guide is a useful starting point for entry, classification, valuation, marking, and importer responsibilities. Other destinations use different authorities and rules.

## Packing list

The packing list describes how the goods are physically arranged. It is used by carriers, warehouses, inspectors, and customs teams to find and reconcile cargo.

Check that it states:

- shipper and consignee;
- purchase order and shipment reference;
- carton, pallet, or package numbering;
- product and variant in each package;
- quantity per package and total quantity;
- net and gross weight;
- package dimensions and total volume where needed;
- shipping marks and handling notes;
- container or seal details once available.

The total in the packing list must reconcile with the invoice, purchase order, inspection report, and booking data. If one document uses sets, another uses pieces, and a third uses cartons, add the conversion clearly.

## Bill of lading or air waybill

The transport document depends on the mode and service. A sea bill of lading and an air waybill have different functions, release procedures, and commercial effects.

Review:

- shipper, consignee, and notify party;
- port or airport of loading and discharge;
- place of receipt and final delivery where shown;
- vessel, flight, voyage, or routing;
- package count and weight;
- marks and concise goods description;
- freight-prepaid or collect instruction where applicable;
- original, telex, electronic, or express-release procedure;
- container and seal numbers;
- document issue date and amendment process.

The buyer, supplier, forwarder, carrier, and broker should know who is authorized to request a correction. An incorrect consignee or release instruction can delay cargo even when the goods are acceptable.

Compare the operational handoff using the [air freight versus sea freight guide](/blog/air-freight-vs-sea-freight-china/).

## Origin, export, and product evidence

Additional documents depend on the product and destination. They may include:

- certificate of origin or origin statement;
- export declaration or customs data;
- material or composition information;
- test or inspection report;
- safety, conformity, or registration evidence;
- fumigation, treatment, or wood-packaging evidence;
- dangerous-goods declaration;
- battery, chemical, or food-contact information;
- insurance certificate;
- letter of credit or bank documents;
- product-specific permits or licenses.

Do not assume that a supplier’s generic certificate proves the exact product is accepted in the buyer’s market. Match the company, factory, model, materials, standard, test method, issue date, and destination requirement. The [product compliance checklist](/blog/china-product-compliance-checklist/) explains how to research the evidence chain.

## Map documents to Incoterms responsibilities

An Incoterms rule does not replace a complete document matrix. For each document, state:

- who prepares it;
- who reviews it;
- who submits it;
- the deadline;
- the required language or format;
- the correction owner;
- the cost and consequence of a late or incorrect document.

For example, an exporter may prepare commercial and export documents while the importer or broker controls destination entry data. The actual split depends on the agreed rule, named place, contract, route, and local procedure.

Use the [Incoterms responsibility matrix](/tools/incoterms-responsibility-matrix/), the [FOB, EXW, and CIF guide](/blog/fob-exw-cif-china-imports/), and the official [ICC Incoterms rules overview](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/incoterms-rules/) to clarify the handoff before the order is ready.

## Perform a pre-departure document review

Set a document cut-off before the booking cut-off. A practical review asks:

1. Does every party name match the contracting and shipping structure?
2. Do product descriptions and quantities match across documents?
3. Do cartons, weights, and dimensions match the final packing list?
4. Is the invoice value supported by the commercial agreement?
5. Are origin, marking, testing, and permit requirements addressed?
6. Does the transport document show the agreed route and release method?
7. Are documents available in the format and timing required by the broker, bank, retailer, or warehouse?
8. Are any assumptions, deviations, or amendments recorded?

Save the reviewed versions. Do not let an email attachment with the same filename silently replace an approved document.

## Common China import document problems

### Product descriptions are too vague

“Accessories” or “gift” may not communicate material, function, or use. Ask the broker what description supports the destination process without inventing a classification.

### The packing list uses a different unit

Reconcile pieces, sets, cartons, and inner packs. Small arithmetic inconsistencies can delay a manual review.

### The named parties do not match

A factory, exporter, contracting company, bank beneficiary, and importer can be different entities, but the relationship must be explained and documented.

### The certificate belongs to another product

Check model, material, factory, and test scope. A logo or supplier name alone is not enough.

### The document arrives after the cargo

Set deadlines backward from customs, payment, warehouse, and delivery needs. A carrier transit estimate does not include time to correct an incomplete document pack.

## Frequently asked questions

### What documents do I need to import goods from China?

Most shipments require a commercial invoice, packing list, and transport document, but the complete set depends on the product, origin, destination, value, mode, broker, payment terms, and local rules. Regulated products can require additional testing, labels, permits, or declarations.

### Does the supplier or buyer prepare the import documents?

Responsibilities depend on the contract, Incoterms rule, named place, customs arrangement, and local law. Write the handoff instead of relying on the phrase “supplier handles shipping.”

### Can I use the supplier’s invoice for customs?

It may be the starting document, but the importer remains responsible for the accuracy and destination requirements that apply to the entry. Review it with the broker and correct discrepancies before filing.

### Should I ask for documents before paying the balance?

Agree which documents are available before release and which can only be issued after departure. Connect the document milestone to the [supplier payment-terms workflow](/blog/china-supplier-payment-terms/) without making an impossible condition.

## Keep the document pack with the shipment evidence

Store the purchase order, approved specification, invoice, packing list, inspection report, booking, transport document, certificates, customs records, and amendments under one shipment reference. That record helps resolve a receiving discrepancy and improves the next order’s RFQ and packing assumptions.

> This guide is general educational information. Customs classification, valuation, origin, permits, taxes, and document requirements vary by product and destination; confirm current requirements with the relevant authorities and qualified customs professionals.
