The lowest China freight quote is not necessarily the lowest shipment cost. Two forwarders can quote the same cargo while including different pickup points, export charges, routing, destination fees, customs services, free time, or delivery scope.

To compare freight forwarder quotes for shipping from China, first make every provider answer the same shipment brief. Then normalize the charge basis, exclusions, transit assumptions, and responsibilities before scoring price.

## Freeze the shipment brief before requesting rates

Send one written request for quotation to every forwarder. Include:

- shipper legal name and pickup address;
- cargo-ready date and operating contact;
- origin port or airport if already determined;
- destination port, airport, warehouse, or delivery postcode;
- product description and intended use;
- number and type of packages;
- gross weight and package dimensions;
- total volume for sea freight;
- pallet status and stackability;
- battery, liquid, magnet, chemical, food-contact, dangerous-goods, or other handling information;
- declared cargo value and currency;
- requested mode and service level;
- agreed Incoterms rule, named place, and edition;
- required insurance, customs, delivery, appointment, or warehouse service;
- quote deadline and desired validity period.

Do not ask a forwarder to guess cargo dimensions from the order value or unit count. If final packing data is unavailable, label the figures as estimates and require a re-quote trigger.

Your [packaging specification](/blog/china-packaging-specification/) should eventually produce the carton dimensions, weights, marks, pallet configuration, and handling constraints used for booking.

## Make the quoted scope explicit

Ask each forwarder to state where its responsibility and price begin and end.

A quote may cover some or all of these stages:

1. factory pickup;
2. origin warehouse or consolidation;
3. export documentation and customs handling;
4. terminal, port, or airport charges;
5. main international carriage;
6. transshipment;
7. destination terminal charges;
8. customs brokerage;
9. duty and tax advance;
10. inspection or agency holds;
11. final delivery and appointment;
12. empty-equipment return or proof of delivery.

“Door to door” is not a standardized charge list. Require the door address, excluded fees, customs assumptions, duty/tax treatment, delivery equipment, waiting-time rules, and unloading responsibility.

Use the [EXW, FOB, and CIF comparison](/blog/fob-exw-cif-china-imports/) to map the seller/buyer delivery responsibilities before asking a forwarder to price the buyer-controlled portion.

## Normalize the charge basis

Put every quote into one comparison currency and one line-item table. Preserve the original currency and exchange-rate assumption so a later invoice can be reconciled.

| Charge group | Questions to normalize |
| --- | --- |
| Pickup | Included address, vehicle, waiting time, loading, tolls, and fuel surcharge |
| Origin | Documentation, customs handling, warehouse, consolidation, terminal, and security fees |
| Main freight | Carrier, mode, chargeable weight or volume, equipment, routing, and surcharge basis |
| Destination | Terminal, deconsolidation, documentation, handling, storage, and release fees |
| Customs | Brokerage scope, tariff-line count, bonds or guarantees, duty/tax advance, and disbursement fee |
| Delivery | Delivery postcode, appointment, residential or limited-access fees, liftgate, waiting, and unloading |
| Optional | Cargo insurance, inspection, relabeling, palletization, and special handling |

For air and courier services, verify how chargeable weight is calculated. For less-than-container-load sea freight, check both volume/weight minimums and destination deconsolidation charges. For full-container service, check equipment type, free time, detention, demurrage, chassis, and delivery conditions relevant to the route.

## Separate fixed, variable, and pass-through charges

Classify each line:

- **Fixed:** quoted per shipment, document, entry, or container.
- **Variable:** based on weight, volume, package count, distance, time, or tariff lines.
- **Conditional:** charged only after an event such as storage, inspection, amendment, waiting, re-delivery, or equipment delay.
- **Pass-through:** carrier, terminal, government, or third-party outlay that may change under stated rules.

A strong quote states the unit, quantity, rate, subtotal, currency, tax treatment, and condition for each line. “Local charges as actual” provides no usable comparison unless the provider lists the likely charge categories and evidence process.

## Compare route and timing, not only transit days

Ask for:

- proposed carrier or service;
- direct or transshipment routing;
- planned origin and destination points;
- estimated departure and arrival;
- cutoff and latest cargo-receipt time;
- frequency of service;
- estimated port-to-port or airport-to-airport transit;
- estimated door-to-door duration;
- known seasonal or capacity constraints;
- what happens if the planned sailing or flight is missed.

A quoted transit time is not the same as total lead time. Pickup, consolidation, customs, terminal dwell, transshipment, destination release, and final-delivery scheduling can materially change the arrival at your warehouse.

Connect the booking cutoff to the [manufacturing and cargo-ready timeline](/blog/china-manufacturing-lead-time/) rather than assuming freight can be arranged after production finishes.

## Check quote validity and re-rating triggers

Freight markets and surcharges change. Every quote should show:

- issue date and expiry;
- rate basis and currency;
- included fuel, security, peak-season, and carrier surcharges;
- exchange-rate assumption;
- cargo-data assumptions;
- re-rating events, such as changed dimensions, delayed cargo, equipment change, new routing, or expired validity;
- cancellation, amendment, and no-show charges.

If a quote will expire before the cargo-ready date, use it as a planning estimate and schedule a rate refresh. Do not present it internally as a fixed landed cost.

## Verify the forwarder and operating chain

Understand which entity issued the quote, receives payment, makes the booking, files export documents, acts at destination, and communicates during exceptions.

Ask for:

- legal company name and business address;
- relevant registrations or licenses for the service and jurisdiction;
- origin and destination office or agent details;
- experience with the product, trade lane, mode, and shipment size;
- dangerous-goods or special-cargo capability where needed;
- cargo-claims process and insurance information;
- escalation contacts and operating hours;
- sample booking confirmation, milestone updates, and invoice format with sensitive data removed.

Do not rely only on a marketplace profile or messaging account. Verify the contracting and payment entity with the same discipline used for a product supplier.

## Build a weighted comparison

Price can be one part of a simple decision score.

| Area | Example questions |
| --- | --- |
| Scope completeness | Are origin, main freight, destination, customs, and delivery boundaries explicit? |
| Cost transparency | Are charge units, assumptions, exclusions, and conditional fees visible? |
| Route suitability | Does the routing match the cargo's urgency, value, and handling risk? |
| Operational capability | Has the provider handled this product, mode, lane, and destination process? |
| Communication | Are milestones, exceptions, and escalation ownership defined? |
| Commercial control | Are validity, payment, claims, and change rules documented? |

Record the reason for selecting a provider. This creates a useful baseline when the first shipment invoice, transit performance, damage record, and communication history are reviewed.

## Complete the booking handoff

After selection, confirm the final shipment data and responsibilities in one controlled handoff:

- booking reference and forwarder contact;
- shipper, consignee, and notify-party details;
- cargo-ready date, pickup window, and address;
- final package count, dimensions, gross/net weight, and volume;
- product and customs-description data;
- Incoterms rule and named place;
- routing and service;
- required commercial invoice, packing list, licenses, certificates, and declarations;
- shipping marks and label files;
- draft transport-document review deadline;
- customs-broker and importer-of-record instructions;
- cargo-insurance decision;
- milestone update and exception protocol.

Feed the confirmed freight scope into the [China landed-cost calculation](/blog/calculate-landed-cost-china/) using separate origin, main-carriage, destination, customs, delivery, and contingency lines.

## Red flags in a China freight quote

- A total price with no route, scope, charge basis, or exclusions.
- “All included” while duty, tax, inspection, storage, and delivery conditions remain undefined.
- A provider chooses the Incoterms rule without confirming the buyer-seller contract.
- The quote uses estimated cargo data but does not state how re-rating works.
- Destination charges are unavailable for an LCL shipment.
- Payment is requested to an unexplained individual or unrelated entity.
- The promised transit time omits pickup, consolidation, customs, or final delivery.
- The provider will not identify its destination agent or claims process.
- A very low headline rate depends on conditional fees that are not capped or evidenced.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many freight forwarder quotes should I compare?

For a new lane or material shipment, several comparable quotes can reveal scope and market differences. The value comes from using one brief and a normalized table, not from collecting many incomparable totals.

### Should I use the supplier's freight forwarder?

It can be practical, but confirm whose interests the forwarder represents, the exact delivery scope, destination charges, document control, and your access to shipment information. Compare it with at least one buyer-controlled option when risk or cost justifies it.

### Does the cheapest quote produce the lowest landed cost?

Not always. Excluded destination fees, long routing, storage exposure, weak documentation, damage, or re-rating can outweigh a lower base rate. Compare the complete expected cost and operational risk.

### When should I request the final freight rate?

Begin route and budget planning before production. Request or refresh the bookable rate when cargo data, cargo-ready timing, and quote validity are sufficiently stable for the selected mode.
