The lowest unit price is often the least comparable number on a supplier quotation. One factory may include retail packaging, testing, export handling, and a better material grade. Another may quote an unfinished product at a different order quantity and delivery point.

Before ranking suppliers, put every quotation on the same commercial and technical basis. The work is less about making a prettier spreadsheet than removing hidden differences.

## Freeze the RFQ before comparing responses

Suppliers cannot quote the same product if they received different requirements. Give each serious candidate one controlled request for quotation containing:

- product drawing or specification revision;
- material, finish, color, and performance requirements;
- estimated quantity by model and variant;
- packaging and labeling scope;
- sample and approval requirements;
- inspection and testing expectations;
- requested delivery rule and named place;
- target production timing;
- quotation-validity and response deadline;
- questions about tooling, MOQ, capacity, and subcontracting.

If a requirement is unknown, label it as an open assumption. A supplier who makes a sensible assumption should state it rather than bury it inside the price.

## Normalize the product scope first

Create a compliance column for every material requirement. Mark each supplier response as confirmed, deviated, unclear, or not answered.

Check especially:

- material grade and thickness;
- dimensions and tolerances;
- surface finish and decoration method;
- key components or approved equivalents;
- function and reliability criteria;
- included accessories and spares;
- retail packaging and master-carton configuration;
- labels, manuals, barcodes, and destination variants.

Do not “equalize” a silent assumption. Send it back as a written clarification. A quote can be commercially attractive precisely because it excludes the feature that makes your product saleable.

## Build a comparable cost sheet

Separate recurring unit costs from one-time and order-level charges.

| Cost group | Examples | How to compare it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | Base unit, components, finish, assembly | Same specification and quantity tier |
| Tooling and setup | Mold, fixture, print plate, programming, setup | Ownership, life, revisions, maintenance, acceptance |
| Packaging | Unit pack, inserts, labels, cartons, pallets | Same pack-out and artwork scope |
| Quality | Samples, inspection, tests, certificates | Same test scope and responsible party |
| Export and delivery | Inland transport, export clearance, terminal charges | Same Incoterms rule and named place |
| Commercial | Bank charges, currency basis, tax assumptions | Same payment route and quote validity |

Convert each quotation into an evaluated cost per usable unit, not only the factory’s line-item price. Include reasonable estimates for freight, duty, brokerage, financing time, expected defects, and unusable or replacement stock in the [landed-cost model](/blog/calculate-landed-cost-china/).

## Compare MOQs by their cause

A quotation may contain several minimums:

- order MOQ;
- material or color MOQ;
- production-run MOQ;
- packaging print MOQ;
- MOQ per size, color, plug, language, or SKU;
- master-carton or pallet multiple.

Ask which minimum creates the limit. If packaging has a higher MOQ than the product, generic packaging with applied labels may be a valid first-order option. If a custom material batch drives the MOQ, changing only the finished quantity will not remove the cost.

Use the [MOQ and unit-cost guide](/blog/moq-negotiation-unit-cost/) to test alternatives without pretending that fixed setup and material commitments disappear.

## Make tooling quotes measurable

Tooling prices can look similar while covering different work. Request:

- tool type, material, cavity count, and intended output where relevant;
- included design review and trial rounds;
- sample quantity per trial;
- measurement or validation report;
- expected maintenance and replacement responsibility;
- ownership, identification, storage, and transfer terms;
- changes included before and after acceptance;
- production unit price after tool approval.

A cheap tool that cannot hold the product tolerance or planned volume is not cheaper. Neither is an expensive tool automatically better without a defined output and acceptance plan.

## Put delivery terms on the same named place

“FOB price” is incomplete without the named port and a common understanding of what the supplier includes. EXW, FCA, FOB, and CIF move costs and responsibilities between parties at different points.

For each quote, record:

- rule and named place or port;
- Incoterms edition;
- export-clearance responsibility;
- pickup or origin charge assumptions;
- main carriage and insurance scope when included;
- cargo-ready address;
- shipment packing and pallet assumptions.

Use the [FOB, EXW, and CIF comparison](/blog/fob-exw-cif-china-imports/) to map responsibilities. If two suppliers use different delivery bases, adjust them before ranking price.

## Compare lead time as a schedule

“25 days” may start after deposit, after sample approval, after artwork approval, or after materials arrive. Ask each supplier for the same milestone structure:

- requirement and artwork freeze;
- deposit or payment trigger;
- material lead time;
- tooling or setup time;
- first-off or pre-production approval;
- mass-production time;
- inspection readiness;
- rework allowance;
- final packing and cargo-ready date.

The [China manufacturing lead-time guide](/blog/china-manufacturing-lead-time/) helps turn one promised duration into a comparable critical path.

## Score evidence, not sales presentation

A quotation is also a test of how the supplier handles controlled information. Note whether the response:

- identifies assumptions and deviations;
- answers technical questions specifically;
- separates one-time and recurring costs;
- explains MOQ constraints;
- provides a realistic schedule;
- names subcontracted critical processes;
- states validity, currency, and payment events;
- returns the correct document revision.

Fast, precise clarification can be more valuable than a polished company deck. Vague agreement followed by repeated repricing is a cost signal.

## Use a weighted comparison, but keep stop conditions

A weighted score can combine specification fit, verified capability, quality controls, timing, communication, commercial terms, and cost. However, do not allow a high total score to cancel a material stop condition.

Examples of stop or escalation points include:

- unresolved legal or bank-beneficiary mismatch;
- refusal to state material or product assumptions;
- no credible process for a critical characteristic;
- unapproved substitution presented as equivalent;
- inability to meet a necessary compliance or traceability requirement;
- payment requested before the buyer can verify the relevant evidence.

Use the [supplier evaluation checklist](/tools/supplier-evaluation-checklist/) to record evidence separately from the quote comparison.

## Run a sample before choosing on price

A quotation shows what a supplier promises; a sample project shows how it interprets revisions, records changes, manages workmanship, and communicates problems.

Give shortlisted suppliers the same sample brief and evaluation sheet. Record actual dimensions, function, appearance, packaging assumptions, lead time, and unanswered questions. Follow the [China product sample workflow](/blog/order-product-samples-from-china/) rather than approving by photographs alone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do Chinese supplier quotes vary so much?

The suppliers may be quoting different materials, processes, quantities, packaging, quality controls, delivery points, or business models. Capability and capacity also affect price. Normalize the scope before treating the difference as margin.

### What should a Chinese supplier quotation include?

At minimum: supplier identity, product and revision, quantity tiers, unit price, currency, tooling and setup charges, packaging, MOQ, sample terms, production timing, payment events, delivery rule with named place, validity, and stated assumptions or exclusions.

### Should I tell suppliers my target price?

A target can help a capable supplier suggest process or specification alternatives, but it should not replace a costed requirement. Ask the supplier to identify what changes at the target and keep the baseline quote visible.

### How many supplier quotes should I compare?

There is no useful universal number. Start broad enough to test the market, then spend detailed comparison time on candidates that pass identity, capability, and requirement-fit checks. Five vague quotes are less useful than three responses on the same controlled RFQ.

## Finish with a written decision record

Keep the original quotes, clarification log, normalized comparison, sample results, verification evidence, and reason for selection. Record accepted deviations and open actions before the purchase order is issued.

The goal is not to prove that one supplier is “best.” It is to show why a specific supplier and commercial structure fit this product, quantity, timing, and risk profile better than the available alternatives.
