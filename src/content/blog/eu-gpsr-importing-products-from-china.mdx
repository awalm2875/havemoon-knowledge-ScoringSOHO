The EU General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR) is not a certificate that a Chinese factory can buy for a product. It is a set of safety and traceability duties shared across manufacturers, importers, distributors, online marketplaces, and other economic operators.

The GPSR has applied since **13 December 2024**. It covers consumer non-food products placed on the EU market when sector-specific Union rules do not fully address the relevant safety risk. Products such as toys, electrical equipment, radio devices, cosmetics, and personal protective equipment may also be subject to more specific legislation.

For an EU business importing from China, the practical question is not simply “Does this supplier have GPSR?” The useful question is: **Can we document the product, its risks, the responsible businesses, its production identity, and the action we would take if it proved unsafe?**

This guide turns that question into a pre-order workflow. It is general educational information, not a product-specific legal assessment.

## Start by assigning your legal role

Write down every business in the transaction and its role before approving a label or paying a deposit:

- the manufacturer;
- the Chinese exporter or trading company;
- the EU importer;
- the EU responsible person where required;
- the distributor or retailer;
- the online marketplace seller;
- any fulfilment service provider.

Under the GPSR, an **importer** is an EU-established person who places a product from a third country on the EU market. The importer has its own duties; buying on DDP terms or using a freight forwarder does not automatically transfer them.

A brand owner also needs to check whether it has become the **manufacturer** for regulatory purposes. If a business places a product on the market under its own name or trademark, or substantially modifies it, its role may be different from that of a reseller distributing the original manufacturer’s product.

Do not leave this decision to the shipping document stage. The role determines which company details, records, checks, and post-market actions are required.

## Determine which product rules apply

GPSR research begins with an exact product definition, not a generic catalogue name. Record:

- intended and reasonably foreseeable use;
- intended users, including whether children may use it;
- materials, coatings, batteries, electronics, radio functions, and software;
- skin, food, water, heat, chemical, or mechanical contact;
- accessories and variants;
- warnings and marketing claims;
- countries and sales channels;
- whether the item is new, used, repaired, or reconditioned.

Then check the relevant EU legislation and official guidance. The GPSR does not replace product-specific rules. A radio product, for example, may need work under radio-equipment rules as well as GPSR duties for risks or obligations not covered by that legislation.

Create a short requirements register with the legal source, product scope, responsible party, evidence, language, effective date, and internal owner. The general [China product compliance research checklist](/blog/china-product-compliance-checklist/) explains how to build this wider register.

## Confirm the EU responsible person before sale

When the manufacturer is not established in the EU, a covered product cannot be placed on the EU market unless an EU-established responsible economic operator is available. Depending on the supply chain, that person may be:

- the EU manufacturer;
- the importer;
- an authorised representative with a written mandate; or
- a fulfilment service provider when the conditions in the regulation are met and no other listed operator exists.

The responsible person is not just an address rented for a label. The role includes checking that relevant technical documentation and safety information exist, cooperating with market-surveillance authorities, and helping with corrective action when necessary.

Before production, collect and verify:

1. the responsible person’s full legal name;
2. its EU postal address;
3. its electronic address;
4. the basis on which it holds the role;
5. any written mandate and its product scope;
6. the contact and escalation process for authority requests or safety incidents.

Confirm that the same information is used consistently on the product, packaging, accompanying document, and distance-sales offer where the applicable rules require it. A marketplace listing that says only “EU REP” without an identifiable legal business is weak evidence.

## Build a GPSR supplier evidence pack

Request evidence against the exact product and revision you intend to buy. A useful pack is organised into the following sections.

### 1. Business identities

Collect the Chinese manufacturer’s legal name, registered address, actual production address, business licence, and the relationship between the factory, exporter, and invoice issuer. Check these details using the [supplier verification workflow](/blog/verify-chinese-supplier/).

Record the EU importer and responsible person separately. Do not substitute a brand name, marketplace nickname, or forwarding warehouse for a legal entity.

### 2. Product identity and traceability

Define a stable product identifier: model, type, item number, batch, serial number, or another code appropriate to the product. Link it to:

- approved drawings and specifications;
- bill of materials and critical components;
- colour, size, electrical, or material variants;
- packaging and artwork revision;
- factory and production line;
- production and inspection lot;
- test reports and technical records.

The objective is to answer two questions later: “Which configuration was assessed?” and “Which shipped units are affected?”

### 3. Risk assessment

Ask for a documented assessment of reasonably foreseeable hazards throughout the product’s expected life. The analysis may include mechanical, electrical, thermal, fire, chemical, hygiene, choking, accessibility, cybersecurity, or misuse risks depending on the product.

The document should show the hazard, exposed user, scenario, severity, likelihood, risk-reduction measure, verification method, and remaining risk. A one-page statement reading “product is safe” is not a risk assessment.

Use qualified compliance professionals and laboratories where the product or applicable legislation requires expertise. The buyer should still understand how the assessed construction matches the ordered product.

### 4. Test and technical evidence

Collect the evidence required by the applicable rules. This may include test reports, declarations, calculations, design records, safety data, material declarations, instructions, and standards used.

For every report, match:

- manufacturer and factory;
- product model and photographs;
- materials and critical components;
- standard and edition;
- sample date and report date;
- complete result pages;
- laboratory identity and relevant accreditation scope;
- later product or supplier changes.

A CE mark or “CE certificate” does not by itself prove GPSR compliance. CE marking only applies where specific EU legislation requires it, and the underlying conformity work must relate to the exact product.

### 5. Labels, warnings, and instructions

Create an artwork matrix rather than reviewing each file in isolation. Depending on the product and applicable law, check:

- manufacturer name and postal and electronic address;
- importer and responsible-person information;
- product, model, batch, or serial identifier;
- required marks and disposal information;
- country-of-origin marking where applicable;
- warnings, age restrictions, and instructions;
- language required in each destination country;
- legibility, permanence, and placement.

Freeze the approved files with revision numbers. Make label or packaging changes subject to written approval in the [China purchase order checklist](/blog/china-purchase-order-checklist/).

### 6. Complaint and corrective-action records

Agree how complaints, accidents, returns, and suspected unsafe products will be recorded and escalated. The file should identify who can:

- stop production or shipment;
- quarantine stock;
- trace affected lots and customers;
- preserve samples and production records;
- investigate root cause;
- contact the responsible person and authorities;
- issue a warning, withdrawal, or recall;
- verify corrective action.

Supplier cooperation clauses are useful, but a contract cannot remove statutory duties from the importer or another EU economic operator.

## Put the controls into the RFQ and purchase order

Compliance should not appear for the first time after the goods are finished. Add measurable requirements to the sourcing documents before price and tooling are locked.

An RFQ or purchase order can require:

- exact product, material, component, and artwork revisions;
- applicable market and intended use;
- named manufacturer and production site;
- approved subcontractors and critical-component sources;
- specified standards, tests, and sample configuration;
- traceability and production-record requirements;
- access to technical and quality evidence;
- written approval before a material, component, process, software, factory, label, or packaging change;
- notice of complaints, incidents, regulator contact, or product-safety concerns;
- support for investigation, withdrawal, and recall;
- inspection and independent-testing rights;
- responsibility for agreed rework and corrective action.

Avoid vague clauses such as “supplier guarantees all EU compliance.” Keep the commercial allocation of responsibility, but attach the actual controlled specifications and evidence list.

## Check the online product listing

GPSR distance-sales information is frequently missed because the factory reviews the physical label while the e-commerce team builds the listing later.

Before an offer goes live, the listing should clearly and visibly provide the information required for the product, including:

- a product image and the information needed to identify it;
- the manufacturer’s name and postal and electronic address;
- when the manufacturer is outside the EU, the responsible person’s name and postal and electronic address;
- applicable warning or safety information in language consumers can understand.

The exact requirement depends on the product and applicable legislation. Keep screenshots or controlled exports of the approved listing. Recheck marketplace templates after catalogue merges, variant changes, translation updates, or seller-account migrations.

Do not hide required safety information only inside a downloadable manual that a customer is unlikely to see before purchase.

## Verify mass production before shipment

Document review does not prove that mass-produced goods match the assessed configuration. Before shipment:

1. reconcile the production bill of materials with the approved version;
2. confirm critical component and material lots;
3. inspect product identifiers, labels, warnings, and instructions;
4. sample functional and safety-related characteristics appropriate to the product;
5. check packaging and traceability codes;
6. record the inspection lot and sample selection;
7. investigate discrepancies before release.

A [pre-shipment inspection](/blog/pre-shipment-inspection-china/) can confirm visible and measurable production requirements, but it does not replace qualified testing or a product-specific legal review.

## Respond when a product may be unsafe

Do not wait for certainty before preserving evidence and stopping further exposure. When a complaint or test result suggests a serious safety issue:

- identify the exact product and affected lots;
- stop sale and shipment while the risk is assessed;
- notify the relevant internal owner, importer, and responsible person;
- preserve samples, listings, test records, and production evidence;
- investigate distribution and customer records;
- obtain qualified advice on reporting and corrective action;
- cooperate with the relevant market-surveillance authority.

The European Commission’s [Safety Business Gateway](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/gpsd/screen/public/home) is the channel businesses use to notify authorities about dangerous non-food consumer products. The notification and action required depend on the facts; prepare the process before an incident occurs.

## Common GPSR mistakes in China sourcing

| Mistake | Why it fails | Better control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asking whether the factory is “GPSR certified” | GPSR is not a general factory certificate | Define the product, roles, risks, evidence, and traceability |
| Treating CE as a universal approval | CE applies only under legislation that requires it | Identify every applicable rule and verify the underlying file |
| Using a report for a similar model | Materials, construction, or hazards may differ | Match report scope to the ordered model and revision |
| Adding an EU address only to the carton | Required information may also belong on the product, documents, and online offer | Use a product-specific information matrix |
| Letting the factory change components without notice | The risk assessment and tests may no longer describe production | Freeze the bill of materials and require change approval |
| Rebranding without reviewing legal roles | A private-label seller may take manufacturer responsibilities | Assign roles before artwork and contracts are approved |
| Leaving the online listing to the marketplace team | Mandatory identity or warning information may be absent at the point of sale | Add a listing approval and screenshot record |

## A 30-minute buyer file check

Before releasing a deposit or production order, ask whether the file can answer each question without relying on a salesperson’s memory:

- Who is the legal manufacturer and where is the product made?
- Who is the EU importer?
- Who is the EU responsible person, and why do they hold that role?
- Which rules apply to this exact product and intended use?
- What model, batch, or serial system identifies the goods?
- What hazards were assessed and how were they controlled?
- Do tests and documents match the approved production revision?
- Are physical labels, instructions, and online information approved?
- Can shipped units be traced to production and customers?
- Who stops sale, reports, investigates, and recalls if necessary?

Any “we will obtain that after shipment” answer should be treated as an open release condition, not completed compliance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does GPSR apply to products imported from China?

It can. GPSR applies to covered consumer products placed on the EU market regardless of where they were made. Product-specific EU legislation may apply as well, so the correct analysis begins with the exact product and risk—not the country of manufacture alone.

### Does every Chinese manufacturer need an EU responsible person?

For a covered product whose manufacturer is outside the EU, an EU-established responsible economic operator must be identified before the product is placed on the EU market. The correct operator depends on the supply chain and may be the importer, an authorised representative, or in defined circumstances a fulfilment service provider. Confirm the role and duties for the specific product.

### What GPSR information must appear in an online product listing?

For distance sales, the offer should provide the product identification, manufacturer identity and contact details, the EU responsible person’s details when the manufacturer is outside the EU, and applicable warnings or safety information. More specific product legislation may add requirements.

### Is CE certification enough for GPSR?

No. CE marking is required only by particular EU harmonisation laws and is supported by the relevant conformity process. It does not replace GPSR role, traceability, online-information, risk, or post-market obligations that apply to the product.

### Who is the manufacturer of a private-label product?

The factory may physically make it, but a business placing a product on the market under its own name or trademark can be treated as the manufacturer under EU product rules. Private-label buyers should determine this before packaging, documentation, and contracts are finalised.
